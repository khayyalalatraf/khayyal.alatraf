The Law, in Plain Terms

Saudi Arabia's updated foreign real estate ownership law took effect in early 2026. Its executive regulation and the exact permitted geographic zones were approved by the Council of Ministers in June 2026.

A legally resident, non-Saudi individual may own one residential property up to 3,000 square meters outside Makkah and Madinah. Ownership is also permitted within designated zones inside Makkah and Madinah themselves, and without restriction (100%) inside special economic zones such as NEOM and Qiddiya. Non-listed Saudi companies with foreign ownership can also acquire property for business purposes under separate conditions.

Eligible zones are defined block by block, not simply by city. We verify whether a specific property falls inside a permitted zone before you commit to it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners own property in Saudi Arabia in 2026?

Yes. Legally resident foreign individuals may own one residential property up to 3,000 m² outside Makkah and Madinah, with ownership also permitted in designated zones within Makkah and Madinah, and unrestricted ownership inside special economic zones like NEOM and Qiddiya.

Which cities are open to foreign ownership?

Riyadh and Jeddah are among the currently permitted areas, alongside specific zones in Makkah, Madinah, and AlUla. Eligibility is zone-specific, not city-wide — we verify each property individually.

Do I need a local agent to complete the purchase?

You don't legally need one, but working with a REGA-licensed establishment like ours ensures your application, financial transfer, and registration are handled correctly and in compliance with the regulation.
